Availability is a complex matter in some regards, and simpler in some others. In one study conducted by the University of Michigan on current college students with mobility impairments, the majority reported having to “think ahead about where they may use the bathroom during the day.”[1] They learned what washroom facilities were suitable for them through experience, while learning to avoid others.[2] Furthermore, universal design calls for making spaces accessible for all regardless of ability with design that aids all and can be used by all, with design keeping all users in mind.[3] Examples of this are cutouts for curbs or ramps to entrances to help wheelchairs as well as strollers and bikes and walkers.[4] However, degraded accessibility and lack of availability of accessible facilities can lead to universal accessibility not living up to this vision, such as a limited number of accessible bathrooms partially used by people without disabilities.[5] Requirement for their availability also shows the importance of maintenance, with the study noting that that “providing readily available, private, well-maintained accessible bathrooms around campus was a key barrier identified by most students” in the study.[6]
