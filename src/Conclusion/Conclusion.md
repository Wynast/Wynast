## Conclusion

                In 2022’s Canadian Survey on Disability, it was reported that there were over 8 million with disabilities, or 27% of the Canadian population aged 15 or over. The majority of these, with over 6 million, are according to statistical data experiencing severe barriers to public toilet accessibility.[\[1\]](#_ftn1) This is thus a substantial portion of the population that can be negatively impacted by insufficient accessible toilet availability. Toilet activities are vial, with the “the ability to perform self-care tasks safely and easily in a hygienic setting . . . valued as a foundation Activity of Daily Life (ADL) and linked to health-related quality of life.”[\[2\]](#_ftn2) A lack of functional accessible toilets not only has emotional impact upon those requiring them, but also poses “health complications and risks to people with disabilities and their carer(s).”[\[3\]](#_ftn3) Furthermore, a lack of public toilet facilities constrains people, potentially restricting them to known ‘safe’ areas with accessible toilets.[\[4\]](#_ftn4) Providing accessible toilet facilities is required to better allow people with disabilities to “fully engage in higher education.”[\[5\]](#_ftn5)
