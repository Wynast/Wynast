The design of a bathroom/washroom/restroom is a major factor in their accessibility. The CSA/ASC B651:23 standard published by Accessibility Standards Canada calls for public washrooms to provide equipment such as an accessible lavatory (sink), stall, accessories, and urinal if applicable.[1] It further calls for signage to indicate washrooms and to guide users to the nearest accessible facilities should the signs be placed for an inaccessible washroom.[2] However, the University of Guelph is provincially regulated and thus abides by provincial regulations.[3] Much of the Ontario Building Code aligns with that of Canadian standards, with for instance signage required to indicate the location of barrier-free washrooms with the signage to follow the design of the aforementioned CSA B651 standard.[4] Design standards and building codes themselves also will have minimal effect as MacKinnon and various other buildings around the campus of the university of Guelph are rather old; in case of existing buildings, they are only subject to the code of when they were built unless extended or major renovations are undertaken, in which case the present code will apply to extensions and majorly renovated portions.[5]

The University of Guelph furthermore maintains its own standards, the Design & Construction Standards. It among other things calls for universal washrooms to have a BlindSquare QR code (2.3.2.1.2.6 pg 2.3.3), universal washrooms to be present on every floor after renovation or in new buildings (2.3.2.2.2.3 pg 2.3.4), washrooms to have standardised signage indicating accessibility (pg 3.5.7), and more.
